## Rousseau and the Concept of General Will
Jean-Jacques Rousseau introduced the concept of the general will as the central doctrine of his political philosophy, arguing that a state is only legitimate when guided by the collective interest of its members. This theory suggests that individuals must subordinate their personal power and person to the direction of the general will to promote the common good. While intended to ensure harmony and liberty, the concept has been criticized because the general will often represents the views of an enlightened few rather than a true majority of the population.

## The Influence of Rousseau on Napoleon
Napoleon Bonaparte was deeply influenced by Rousseau's ideas regarding the general good and the role of the state. However, the relationship between the two was complex; after seizing power in the coup of 18 Brumaire, Napoleon expressed sarcasm toward the revolutionary ideals he had once inherited. Despite this, his attempt to reshape Europe and his rise to power are often viewed through the lens of the political shifts initiated by Rousseau's theories on democracy and participation.

## Rousseau as a Precursor to Totalitarianism
Scholars have frequently debated whether Rousseau serves as the father of totalitarianism, with some arguing that his philosophy popularized an expanded role for the state in modern society. Critics suggest that the demand for total community autonomy and the suppression of individual interests in favor of the general will laid the groundwork for twentieth-century dictatorships. This interpretation links Rousseau's rationalist liberalism to the later emergence of absolute state control seen in regimes led by figures like Hitler.

## Connections to Hitler and Modern Dictators
Political theorists have explicitly identified Adolf Hitler as a historical outcome of the philosophical trajectory started by Rousseau. While Rousseau envisioned a harmony of free peoples and the end of military empires, his theories were converted into totalitarian systems by his successors. This transition highlights a fundamental difference between the original rationalist intent of Enlightenment thinkers and the actual implementation of state power by totalitarian leaders who claim to represent the collective will.
