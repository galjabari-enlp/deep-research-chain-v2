## Official Election Outcomes and Electoral Totals
The 2020 United States presidential election resulted in a victory for the Democratic ticket of Joe Biden and Kamala Harris. According to official records from the Federal Election Commission and the National Archives, Joe Biden secured 306 electoral votes, surpassing the 270-vote majority required to win the presidency. His main opponent, incumbent Donald J. Trump, received 232 electoral votes. This outcome was solidified when Pennsylvania's 20 electoral votes pushed Biden over the necessary threshold to become the 46th President of the United States.

## State Certification and Verification Process
The election process involves a rigorous sequence of counting ballots, canvassing results, and official certification by state authorities. By December 11, 2020, all 50 states and the District of Columbia had completed the certification of their general election results. This certification step is a critical legal requirement where election officials verify the accuracy of the vote counts before the results are finalized and sent to federal authorities.

## Congressional Count and Final Confirmation
The final stage of the presidential election occurred during a joint session of the 117th United States Congress on January 6–7, 2021. Pursuant to the Electoral Count Act, Congress met to count the Electoral College ballots submitted by the states. This session officially confirmed the electoral votes as recorded in the Certificates of Ascertainment and Vote, formally declaring Joe Biden the winner of the election despite the scrutiny and unofficial certificates submitted during the aftermath of the vote.
